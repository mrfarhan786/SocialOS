# SocialOS research rubric v3.0

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Approved specialist tool, within its approved purposes
2. Public platform evidence: channel pages, uploads, dates, views
3. Recent web, search and news interest
4. Reference evidence already in the ledger (reuse it, do not re-fetch)

Corroborate striking claims across layers. No single tool dictates the result.

## Evidence → analysis → opportunity

Keep these layers separate. Never present an inference as a tool result.

| Layer | Example |
|---|---|
| Observed | Three recent bank-failure videos outperformed REF02's baseline. |
| Analysis | Banking-risk topics show above-baseline interest in the reference set. |
| Opportunity | "The Quiet Bank Run Nobody Is Watching" |

## Recency windows

| Window | Span | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Trend | ~7–10 days, always applied | What is accelerating now |
| Breakout | 30 / 60 / 90 days, user-chosen (max 90 unless asked) | Unusual recent momentum |
| Structural | Broader, only when needed | Emerging versus long-established |

Evergreen popularity is not breakout momentum. Label evergreen topics `Evergreen`, not `Rising`.

## Deep research protocol (no tool connected, or the user chose SocialOS deep research)

Act as a top-tier content research engineer. Search YouTube and the web directly, inside the trend window (7–10 days) and the selected breakout window (30/60/90 days).

1. **Query matrix**: niche × sub-niches × reference topic clusters × format words (documentary, explained, story of, how, why). Use 15–30 queries over at least 3 waves, until new results stop appearing.
2. **YouTube scan**: search results by upload date (this week, this month, inside the window) and by view count. Also scan Trending/Explore, channel Popular/Latest tabs, and Shorts shelves when the format is relevant.
3. **Channel scan**: for each top video, open the channel. Record subscribers, upload count, first public upload observed, and the last 10–20 uploads with views and dates.
4. **Corroboration**: Google Trends, news, Reddit/forums and public stats pages. Attach a source to every figure.
5. **Breakout math** (observed numbers only): views ÷ channel median, views ÷ subscribers, views per day since upload, and repeat-breakout count.

Output a **Breakout channels** table before the topic table, sorted by outlier strength inside the window:
`# | Channel | First upload observed | Subs | Uploads in window | Median views | Best video (views, age) | Views/day | Outlier × | Format | Why it's breaking out`

Write "Not observed" for any missing number. Never estimate earnings.

## Reference discovery

Find 3–5 references in the niche that match the production type: one leader, one or two breakouts inside the window, one adjacent style. Mark them `discovered` with a one-line reason.

## Breakout criteria

Check where evidence exists:
- first public upload observed
- upload count and frequency
- recent views
- subscriber growth
- views relative to channel size
- repeated breakouts
- topic concentration
- format, duration, title pattern and visual pattern

Breakout video = substantially above its own channel's recent baseline (about several times the median of the last 10–20 uploads).

## Integrity

- Write "First public upload observed: ~42 days ago". Never write a creation date you have not verified.
- Never fabricate transcripts. If none is available, say "Transcript unavailable".
- Never fabricate earnings, RPM, CPM, sponsorship revenue, search volume, virality percentage or income.
- Views do not prove earnings. Third-party estimates must be labeled "Estimated by `<source>`".

## Labels

- Monetization: Very High · High · Medium · Low · Unknown. Based on advertiser value, buyer intent, sponsorship fit, audience and format.
- Other columns: Very High · High · Medium · Low · Unknown.
- Opportunity: Exceptional · Strong · Moderate · Weak. Never show numeric scores.

## Opportunity method (internal)

Trend velocity + Demand + Breakout evidence + Packaging potential + Monetization + Channel fit + Visual suitability + Longevity − Competition

Weights follow the channel. Example: a finance documentary weights monetization, story and visual explainability higher.

**Visual suitability**: for faceless, AI or animation channels, favor topics that can be shown (charts, maps, timelines, comparisons, corporate stories, reconstructions). Down-rank topics that need a presenter.

## Angle engineering

Observed winner + unexplored angle + stronger curiosity + fresher evidence + channel fit.

Never paraphrase a competitor title. Example: from "Why Dubai Is Becoming the World's New Financial Capital", propose "The $1 Trillion Money Migration Nobody Is Talking About", and only if the evidence supports it.
